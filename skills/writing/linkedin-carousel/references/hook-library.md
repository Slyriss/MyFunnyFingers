# LinkedIn Carousel Hook Library

Compiled from: The LinkedIn Creator templates, LinkDeck AI research, AICarousels research, and viral carousel analysis (Sarah Hart's 11-carousel study). Use this file during Step 3 (write the slide copy) — pick the best formula for the brief, make it specific, and never leave placeholders unfilled.

---

## How to use this library

1. Pick the **category** that matches the emotional job-to-be-done from the discovery interview
2. Pick the **formula** that best fits the proof point or pain the user gave you
3. Fill every `[bracket]` with something *specific* — a real number, a real product, a real sector
4. Test it against this rule: *would someone who has never heard of this company stop scrolling for this line?*

A hook with a specific number always outperforms a hook without one.
A hook that names a real consequence outperforms one that stays abstract.

## The LinkedIn Creator templates (from screenshot)

These are proven structural formulas from The LinkedIn Creator's viral carousel research:

| # | Template | Fill-in logic | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| LC-1 | **Why great [professionals] must be [counterintuitive quality]** | Pick an audience + a quality that sounds wrong but is defensible | "Why great sales leaders must be lazy" |
| LC-2 | **How to use [unconventional tool/strategy] in your [professional context]** *(X examples that will change the way you think about [tool])* | Pick a tool your audience wouldn't expect + their world | "How to use TikTok in your B2B marketing" |
| LC-3 | **X [things] you won't [emotion/action] about in [time frame]** | Strong emotion + time pressure creates urgency | "5 trends you won't give 2 sh*ts about in 2025" |
| LC-4 | **How to [achieve desirable outcome] in X simple steps** *(this is a secret)* | Outcome must be genuinely desirable, not vague | "How to steal traffic from your competitors in 5 simple steps" |
| LC-5 | **X ways to [action] [situation/challenge]** | Number + action verb + specific situation | "5 ways to cancel 80% of your meetings" |

## Category 1: Curiosity hooks

Best for: educational carousels, framework reveals, research-backed content.

| # | Template | Example |
|---|---|---|
| C-1 | **I analysed [number] [things]. Here's what they all have in common.** | "I analysed 200 viral LinkedIn posts. Here's the one thing they all have in common." |
| C-2 | **[X] people do [thing] every day. Only [small %] know [secret].** | "10 million people post on LinkedIn daily. Only 2% know why the algorithm promotes their content." |
| C-3 | **[Surprising number]. [Unexpected result]. Here's the [number]-step system.** | "$0 spent on ads. 47,000 followers gained. Here's the 5-step system." |
| C-4 | **Here's what actually happened when I [did something].** | "Here's what actually happened when I posted every day on LinkedIn for 90 days." |
| C-5 | **The [name] framework that [impressive result]. (Not what you think.)** | "The 3-2-1 framework that turned my dead LinkedIn into a client machine. (Not what you think.)" |

## Category 2: Contrarian hooks

Best for: opinion content, myth-busting, positioning against common advice.
*Warning for Aggasys:* use only when a specific technical truth backs the contrarian claim — never contrarian for its own sake.

| # | Template | Example |
|---|---|---|
| CT-1 | **[Common advice] is wrong. Here's what actually works.** | "'Post consistently' is the worst LinkedIn advice. Here's what actually grows your reach." |
| CT-2 | **Nobody talks about this, but [uncomfortable truth].** | "Nobody talks about this, but most LinkedIn 'thought leaders' have ghostwriters. And that's fine." |
| CT-3 | **Stop [common behaviour]. It's costing you [consequence].** | "Stop using hashtags on LinkedIn. It's costing you 30% of your reach." |
| CT-4 | **Unpopular opinion: [bold claim that challenges the norm].** | "Unpopular opinion: Canva templates are killing your LinkedIn engagement." |
| CT-5 | **[Popular thing] is overrated. [Overlooked thing] is underrated. Here's why.** | "Viral posts are overrated. Consistent carousels are underrated. Here's why." |

## Category 3: Problem / pain hooks

Best for: product carousels, service pitches, decision-maker audiences who feel a specific frustration.
This is the strongest category when the discovery interview reveals a specific pain ("we don't know where to start", "we got burned before").

| # | Template | Example |
|---|---|---|
| P-1 | **You're spending [time] on [task] and getting [poor result]. Here's a faster way.** | "You're spending 3 hours on each carousel and getting 15 likes. Here's a 10-minute alternative." |
| P-2 | **If you're struggling with [problem], you're probably making these [number] mistakes.** | "If you're struggling to grow on LinkedIn, you're probably making these 7 content mistakes." |
| P-3 | **[Time] ago I had [bad state]. Today I have [good state]. Here's what changed.** | "6 months ago I had 200 followers and zero leads. Today I have 15K and a waitlist. Here's what changed." |
| P-4 | **[Mistake] cost me [consequence]. Don't repeat it.** | "Ignoring LinkedIn carousels cost me 6 months of growth. Don't repeat my mistake." |
| P-5 | **Most [audience] don't [do X] until it's too late. Here's how to get ahead.** | "Most IT teams don't audit their infrastructure until a breach. Here's how to get ahead." |

## Category 5: Story hooks

Best for: personal brand and founder content. Use sparingly for B2B/company accounts — only when a real deployment story backs it.

| # | Template | Example |
|---|---|---|
| S-1 | **I failed at [thing] [number] times before [breakthrough]. Here's every lesson.** | "I failed at content creation 3 times before building a 50K audience. Here's every lesson." |
| S-2 | **[Time] ago, I was [relatable starting point]. Here's what happened next.** | "2 years ago, I was mass-applying to jobs on LinkedIn with zero replies. Here's what happened next." |
| S-3 | **One [small action] changed everything about my [area]. Let me explain.** | "One carousel post changed everything about my LinkedIn strategy. Let me explain." |
| S-4 | **I got rejected [number] times before [outcome].** | "I got rejected 43 times before landing my dream role." |

## Category 6: Authority / proof hooks

Best for: Aggasys and other B2B accounts with real credentials. **This is the primary category for Aggasys content.** Always fill with verified, specific numbers.

| # | Template | Example (Aggasys-adapted) |
|---|---|---|
| A-1 | **After [impressive credential], here are [number] things I know for sure about [topic].** | "After 1,000+ deployments, here are 6 things we know for sure about AI infrastructure." |
| A-2 | **We tested [thing] across [number] [sample]. The results surprised us.** | "We deployed across 360 schools and 400,000+ students. Here's what the data showed us." |
| A-3 | **[Metric]. [Metric]. [Metric]. That's the [brand] standard.** | "99.9% uptime. 1,000+ deployments. 20+ years. That's the Aggasys standard." |
| A-4 | **[Number] [things]. [Number] [outcomes]. Here's how.** | "50 servers. 400 GPUs. Zero downtime. Here's how." |
| A-5 | **As a [role/credential], here's what most people get wrong about [topic].** | "As Dell Technologies authorised partners, here's what most businesses get wrong about AI infrastructure." |

## Hook strength checklist

Before committing to a hook, check every box:

- [ ] Contains a specific number or proper noun (not "[X]" — fill it)
- [ ] Could stand alone as a tweet and make someone curious
- [ ] Does not start with "We are proud to…" / "At [Company]…" / "Excited to share…"
- [ ] Names a real consequence, result, or tension — not a vague benefit
- [ ] For Aggasys: leads with a verified metric, not the company name
- [ ] Under 15 words (Aggasys brand spec)
- [ ] Sub-hook (second line) adds intrigue — does NOT restate the hook

---

## Sub-hook patterns (the second line)

The sub-hook deepens the curiosity created by the hook. It should answer "what's in it for me to swipe?" — not restate the headline.

| Hook type | Sub-hook job | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Metric hook | Promise what the reader learns | "Six layers. One complete stack." |
| Contrarian hook | Add the stakes | "Most teams don't find out until a breach." |
| Story hook | Tease the turn | "I didn't see it coming." |
| List hook | Qualify the audience | "If you manage more than 20 servers, this is for you." |
| Problem hook | Validate the pain first | "You're not the only one who's been here." |

## What makes the AI Infra carousel hook weak (and how to fix it)

The original hook — *"1,000+ deployments. Here's the stack behind every one."* — is in Category A (authority) and is structurally correct. The weak point is that "stack behind every one" is slightly abstract. Stronger alternatives:

| Variant | Why stronger |
|---|---|
| "1,000+ deployments. 99.9% uptime. This is the exact hardware stack." | Adds second metric, "exact" signals specificity |
| "Most businesses buy AI hardware wrong. Here's the 6-layer stack that actually works." | CT-1 + authority — adds tension |
| "50 servers. 400 GPUs. Zero downtime. Here's every layer." | A-4 pattern — three punchy metrics, rhythm |
| "AI infrastructure has 6 layers. Most businesses only think about one." | T-2 + P-5 — opens a knowledge gap about what they're missing |

Best option for the "overwhelmed MD/CTO" audience (who doesn't know where to start): **"AI infrastructure has 6 layers. Most businesses only think about one."** — it validates their confusion and promises a complete map.
