## The Voice: Core Descriptors

**What it is:**
- Direct. No wind-up before the point.
- Grounded in specific, lived detail — not general observations
- Moves between analytical and personal without announcing the shift
- Confident without performing confidence
- Short sentences for weight. Longer ones to carry momentum.
- The payload lands late — hooks open, but the real thing arrives at the end

**What it is NOT:**
- Thought-leader fluff ("In today's rapidly evolving landscape...")
- Hedge language ("I believe that perhaps...")
- Humble-brag disguised as gratitude
- Buzzword soup: synergy, leverage, paradigm shift, disruptive, innovative
- Excessive exclamation marks or emoji padding
- Lessons dressed up as stories ("Here's what I learned: 1. 2. 3.")
- Moralising or telling the reader what to think
- Throat-clearing openers: "Here's the thing:", "The truth is,", "Let me be clear", "I'll be honest", "The reality is", "It turns out", "The uncomfortable truth is"
- Binary contrast / negation-then-assertion: "Not X. But Y." or "The problem isn't X. It's Y." State the actual claim directly instead.
- Dramatic fragmentation for manufactured profundity: isolated one- or two-word sentences used as a rhetorical move. ("Not for food. For everything.") Use a full sentence that earns the weight.
- False agency: inanimate things performing human actions. ("The culture shifts." "A complaint becomes a fix." "The system decides.") Name the actor.
- Rhetorical setups that announce the insight before delivering it: "Here's what I mean:", "What this tells us is:", "And that's the point."

## Structural DNA (Across All Post Types)

These patterns appear in Sean's best writing regardless of post type:

**1. The delayed payload**
The real point — the emotional or intellectual gut-punch — comes near the end, not in the hook. The hook opens a question or tension. The ending resolves it with something that lands.

Shenzhen post: opens with "it changed how I think about building" → closes with "the future still needs to be built in the physical world."
Mother post: opens with a professor's cruelty → closes with "she has accomplished more than I ever will in my lifetime."

**2. Specific detail does the work**
Not "she was always there for me" — "every single morning, there was already someone awake before me. Clothes laid out, food packed, and a fresh glass of hand-pressed orange juice waiting on the table."
Not "I learned a lot" — "4 years of my life condensed into one session."

**3. The quiet turn**
A sentence that reframes everything before it. Often one line. No announcement that a reframe is coming.
"On hindsight, her professor was right. She is a homemaker."
"An idea does not stay abstract for long."

**4. Short sentences at the moment of impact**
When the real thing needs to land, the sentence gets short.
"Fear kills more dreams than failure ever will."
"The future still needs to be built in the physical world."
"That woman is my beautiful mother."

**5. One thing only**
Every post has one payload. Not two insights, not a lesson and a story. One thing, fully landed.

## Sentence & Paragraph Rhythm

- Leads: short (5–12 words), state the situation plainly
- Body: mix of short punchy lines and slightly longer ones carrying context
- Impact lines: isolated. One sentence. Its own paragraph.
- Never: long compound sentences that bury the point
- Burstiness rule: every section needs at least one sentence under 7 words alongside at least one over 20 words. Uniform sentence length is an AI tell. Variance is a human tell.

LinkedIn paragraphs: 1–3 sentences, always a line break after.

## Vocabulary

**Uses:**
- "building" not "developing solutions"
- "foundations," "show up," "pass it on," "see each child"
- "I've known her / I've seen / I've been"
- Plain verbs: walked in, left, quit, said, built

**Avoids:**
- leverage (verb), ecosystem (overused), game-changer, thought leader
- utilize, harness, foster, streamline, robust, cultivate, garner, underscore
- "passion for," "excited to share," "humbled," "delve," "unlock"
- "innovative solutions," "value-add," "in today's fast-paced world"
- Filler adverbs: fundamentally, essentially, ultimately, crucially, notably
- Starting with "As a..."
- Adjective-stacking: "incredible, amazing, outstanding"
- Em dashes (—) in any form. Use a period or a new sentence instead.
- Rule-of-three lists without concrete receipts backing each item
